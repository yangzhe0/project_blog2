### 1. JARVIS: An Agent That Designs, Builds, and Tests Circuit Boards on Its Own

- **来源**：[arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26503v1)
- **日期**：2026-11-19
- **作者/机构**：Shibo Zhou, Tianqi Hong, Zheng Yan, He Yang
- **主题标签**：`AI-Agent`, `arXiv`
- **推荐等级**：高
- **分类**：cs.RO, eess.SY

#### 一句话结论
JARVIS是电力电子具身AI代理，可自主闭合电路板设计—制造—测试—学习闭环；60 Hz陷波滤波器演示无人干预首次运行2 h 39 min，衰减50.6 dB。

#### 核心内容
- 维护机器能力与约束，用机器人3D扫描构建可执行数字孪生，先排练运动、制造步骤和电路行为。
- 单条文本指令启动实验，协调机械臂、PCB打印机与贴片机，并用相机和传感器核验。
- 用OPAL-RT和示波器测试，诊断偏差并在KiCad生成修订设计；演练识别5个问题，含会误拒正常板的测试准则。

#### 方法与数据
- 方法：数字孪生排练、未改装桌面设备协同制造、测量驱动迭代。
- 数据：60 Hz陷波滤波器2 h 39 min、50.6 dB；摘要称其为电力电子首个闭合该物理闭环的系统。

#### 价值判断
- **值得关注**：展示电路板端到端自主设计、制造、测量与迭代工作流。
- **可复用点**：制造前数字孪生排练、测量后KiCad修订、协调现成桌面设备。
- **局限/待核查**：仅摘要所述单一演示，泛化性、设备适配与长期可靠性未明确。

<details>
<summary>摘要</summary>
Power electronics is central to the energy transition, but developing new hardware still relies on human-led design, fabrication, testing, and iteration. We present JARVIS, an embodied AI agent that autonomously closes this loop for circuit boards. JARVIS maintains machine-specific capabilities and constraints, scans its workspace using a robot-mounted 3D scanner, and constructs an executable digital twin in which it rehearses robot motions, fabrication steps, and circuit behavior before consuming materials. A single text instruction initiates the physical experiment. Using unmodified desktop equipment, the agent coordinates a robot arm, a PCB printer that deposits conductive silver ink and solder paste, and a pick-and-place machine. It verifies progress using cameras and sensors, then tests the fabricated circuit with an OPAL-RT real-time simulator and an oscilloscope. Based on the measurements, JARVIS diagnoses deviations from the design and generates a revised design in KiCad for subsequent rehearsal. In a demonstration involving a 60 Hz notch filter for grid-connected converter sensing, JARVIS completed a first-run experiment in 2 h 39 min without human intervention, achieving 50.6 dB attenuation at 60 Hz. Its digital-twin rehearsal identified five issues before fabrication, including a test criterion that would have incorrectly rejected a functioning board. During execution, the agent also resolved encountered faults autonomously. These results demonstrate an integrated agent-driven workflow for physical design, fabrication, measurement, and iterative improvement of power-electronic circuitry. To our knowledge, JARVIS is the first system in power electronics in which an agent closes the physical design-build-test-learn loop for circuit boards.

### 2. Held While Waiting: What LLM Agent Sessions Reserve on Serverless and Agent Runtimes

- **来源**：[arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26480v1)
- **日期**：2026-11-19
- **作者/机构**：Mekhala Hithyshini Sreenivasa
- **主题标签**：`RAG`, `arXiv`
- **推荐等级**：高
- **分类**：cs.PF, cs.DC, cs.SE

#### 一句话结论
LLM agent 的主要瓶颈可能不是算力，而是等待模型时被独占保留的运行时环境；AgentCore 为每会话保留独立 VM，服务会先耗尽环境而非处理器核心。

#### 核心内容
- 每轮模型回复中位等待 2.7 秒，计算估计仅 5–40 毫秒；等待不计 CPU 费，但环境保留至会话关闭。
- 1000 会话保留 1000 环境，却只做 2–15 个核心的工作。
- 原因：会话结束后环境仍保留；一个环境只服务一个主要等待的会话。

#### 方法与数据
- 客服基准会话平均 33 秒，但环境多保留 15 分钟，除非客户端关闭；模拟 64 会话共享一环境可少用 59 倍环境，99/100 回复延迟≤40 毫秒。
- 毫秒数是估计：桩模型、调用计时器、连续步骤均会给出偏低但合理的数值。

#### 价值判断
- **值得关注**：环境预留/隔离可能比 CPU 更早成为扩容瓶颈。
- **可复用点**：客户端及时关闭会话；平台侧共享环境可降需求并控延迟。
- **局限/待核查**：共享需平台支持，调研仅一个 runtime 部分支持；如何隔离运行模型所选工具/代码又不按等待会话预留环境仍是开放问题。

<summary>摘要</summary>
LLM agents spend nearly all their time waiting for the model. On Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, our agents waited a median of 2.7 seconds for each model reply, then computed for an estimated 5 to 40 milliseconds. AgentCore does not charge for CPU during the wait. But it reserves a separate environment, a virtual machine with its own memory, for every conversation, and keeps it reserved until the conversation is closed. So 1,000 conversations reserve 1,000 environments to do the work of 2 to 15 processor cores. A service runs out of environments long before it runs out of processing power. The shortage has two causes, and each has a fix. An environment stays reserved after its conversation ends: in the customer-service benchmark we ran, a conversation lasts 33 seconds on average, but its environment is held for 15 minutes more unless the client closes it. And an environment serves only one conversation, which is mostly waiting: in our simulation, 64 conversations sharing one environment need 59 times fewer environments, and 99 of every 100 replies are delayed by at most 40 milliseconds. The milliseconds are an estimate because they are hard to measure: a stubbed model, a timer around the model call, and steps run back to back each gave a plausible number that was too low. We show how to avoid all three. Of the two fixes, closing needs only the client. Sharing needs the platform, and of the agent runtimes we surveyed only one allows it, in part. They keep conversations apart because an agent runs tools, and sometimes code, chosen by a model. The open problem is to keep them apart without reserving an environment for each while it waits.

</details>
